1 · Executive Summary

What you're doing: Launching a two-sided marketplace where the same problem lives on both sides — venues need reliable acts, artists need paid gigs. Your differentiator is that you handle the money (Stripe, contracts, auto-payouts, 1099-K) which every free tool (Facebook groups, group texts) doesn't. That's your wedge.

What NOT to try: "Go viral on TikTok." National TV. Product Hunt as your primary channel. This is a local, physical-world marketplace — venues have addresses, artists show up in person. Concentrate geographically. Ventura County + LA metro is your beachhead. Beat the free tools there before you touch Denver or Nashville.

The strategy in one sentence: Personally recruit the first 30 venues + 100 artists in your 25-mile radius using free trial as bait, use their real gigs as content, then amplify with paid ads once you have proof.

Budget: $2,000 – $3,500 over 90 days is enough. Zero is possible but slower. See §13 for allocation.

Your unfair advantage: You're a musician yourself (Fridays Past, Fifty Proof). You know venue owners. You know artists. You know the exact frustration this solves. Every time someone asks "why does this exist?", the answer is your own booking story. Lead with it. Nobody launching a marketplace has half the founder credibility you do.

2 · The Math & Realistic 90-Day Goals

Marketplaces need liquidity. On the venue side, you need enough active venues that an artist opening the calendar sees gigs. On the artist side, you need enough artists that a venue posting a gig gets 3+ candidates within 48 hours. Those two thresholds are your real launch — user counts alone don't matter.

Reach vs. conversions. "Thousands of people in front of the brand" happens by Day 30 through ads + social + press. Paying, active users follow ~30 days later. Don't confuse impressions with signups.

The one metric that predicts survival: % of posted gigs that get booked through GigsFill within 7 days. Anything above 40% and the flywheel spins. Below 20% and your artist supply is too thin — recruit more artists before more venues.

3 · Pre-Launch Checklist (Days −3 to 0)

Three days of prep before you tell anyone. Skip this and you'll be building the plane while it's flying.

Analytics + tracking

  • Install Google Analytics 4 on gigsfill.com — track pageviews, signups, gig posts, gig bookings as events
  • Install Meta Pixel — required for retargeting ads later. Setup takes 15 minutes at business.facebook.com
  • Add a UTM tag scheme: every link you share must include ?utm_source=X&utm_medium=Y&utm_campaign=Z. Free UTM builder: ga-dev-tools.google/campaign-url-builder
  • Create a Notion or Google Sheet dashboard tracking daily signups by source (see §12 for template)

Social + owned channels

  • Instagram business account: @gigsfill or @gigsfill.com. Logo profile pic. Bio: "Book local live music. Get paid same-week. Ventura & LA area." Link in bio: gigsfill.com
  • Facebook business page: same handle, same bio. Enable "Send Message" CTA.
  • LinkedIn company page: for venue-owner reach. Add yourself as founder.
  • TikTok account: reserve the handle even if you won't post yet. Someone will squat it if you don't.
  • Google Business Profile: register gigsfill.com as a business. Shows up in local searches.

Assets you'll need immediately

  • Logo variants: square (Instagram), rectangular (LinkedIn), on transparent + white background
  • Brand colors documented: purple #7C6BFF, cyan #06B6D4, dark #0F1419. Use these across every visual for recognition.
  • Photo shoot at 1 real gig: Fridays Past or Fifty Proof at 14 Cannons. 20–30 candid photos of the band, crowd, venue exterior, stage lights. This is your content bank for the next 60 days.
  • Screenshots of the app: 8–10 crisp screenshots (calendar, gig modal, payment tab, booking flow, tax center). Blur any real names.
  • One-pager PDF for venues: the demo cheat sheet we built earlier — pack them into every in-person meeting.
  • Business cards: cheap, 250 count on Moo.com or Vistaprint. QR code → gigsfill.com. ~$40.

Product prep

  • Verify Stripe 1099-K toggle is ON (you did this)
  • Verify Free Trial toggle for new venues works — venue can sign up, post gigs, get booked without adding a card
  • Create 3 "seed venues" in the DB — real Ventura County venues you have relationships with, pre-configured with the "Free Trial" flag ON so their first artist doesn't hit payment friction
  • Create 5–10 "seed artists" — friends who agree to be in the initial artist pool so venues see supply from day 1

4 · Phase 1 — Local Beachhead (Days 1–14)

This is the entire launch. Nail the beachhead in 14 days and everything after amplifies. Fumble it and no amount of ad spend saves you.

Rule: Every venue and every artist in these 14 days gets a personal touch. Text, DM, phone call, or in-person. No cold-launched signup form. Marketplaces die when the first users feel like they landed on an empty parking lot.

Day 1 — Launch Day

  • 7:00 AM — Coffee. Open the Notion dashboard. Deep breath.
  • 8:00 AM — Post Launch Announcement (Post #1) to Instagram + Facebook + LinkedIn simultaneously. Same copy, native to each platform.
  • 9:00 AM — Send Personal Network Email (Template A) to 30-50 people who know you personally. BCC everyone; make it feel personal.
  • 10:30 AM — Post the same launch message in these local FB groups (join if not already): "Ventura County Musicians", "LA Live Music Scene", "Conejo Valley Music", "Thousand Oaks Community", "Ventura County Small Business Network". Adjust wording slightly per group.
  • 12:00 PM — Lunch. Then hit "Product Hunt" — submit gigsfill.com (schedule for 12:01 AM Pacific tomorrow). Ask 10 friends via text to upvote at 8 AM tomorrow.
  • 2:00 PM — In-person: visit 3 venues in your 15-mile radius. Not the big-name ones yet — the small bar or restaurant that hosts music 1–2 nights/week. Bring business cards + demo PDF. Ask for the owner or GM. Don't try to close; introduce yourself and hand them the sheet.
  • 5:00 PM — Post an Instagram Story (24hr): candid photo of one of the venues you visited (with permission), text overlay: "Welcoming @[venue] to the GigsFill family 🎸"
  • 7:00 PM — Update dashboard. Log everyone you spoke to. Note follow-ups.

Days 2–5 — Personal outreach sprint

Daily rhythm:

  • Morning — 30 min of DMs. Reply to Day-1 comments. Send follow-up notes to yesterday's venue visits.
  • Late morning — Send 10 personalized Artist DMs (Template B) to local artists you know (via Facebook, Instagram, personal contacts). Not a mass blast — actually mention their band name and a specific gig they've played.
  • Afternoon — Visit 4 more venues. Different neighborhoods each day.
  • Evening — Post 1 piece of content from Content Bank. Pick from post #2 (venue spotlight) or #3 (artist spotlight).
  • Day 3 also: submit story pitch to Ventura County Star ("Local musician launches booking platform for Ventura venues") — see press pitch template.

Days 6–7 — First weekend, first live post

Attend a live gig at one of your seed venues Friday or Saturday night. Not to network — to make content. Shoot:

  • A 10-second Reel of the crowd and band (great B-roll for months)
  • A photo of the artist's paycheck confirmation email from GigsFill on your phone with the venue lights blurred behind — visceral proof the platform works
  • A short interview clip (with venue owner or artist): "Why GigsFill?" — 30 seconds max

Post Reel Saturday morning (best engagement window: 9–11 AM local). Cross-post to Facebook Reels & TikTok.

Days 8–14 — Iterate + amplify

  • Every day: 3 venue visits + 10 artist DMs + 1 social post
  • Day 10: post Testimonial post from the first venue that's booked a gig (get their permission first — free t-shirt or Stripe credit if they'll do a 30-second video)
  • Day 12: submit to Chamber of Commerce (Thousand Oaks & Ventura). ~$300 membership but includes networking events + newsletter placement
  • Day 14: pause. Review dashboard. If you're above the Day-14 target (10 venues / 40 artists), move to Phase 2. If not, add 3 more days of personal outreach before advancing.

6 · Phase 3 — Paid Amplification (Days 30–60)

Turn on paid ads only after you have real testimonials + platform activity to point at. Ads without proof are cold-water expensive.

Meta Ads (Instagram + Facebook)

Run 2 campaigns simultaneously. Each has a specific goal.

Campaign A — Venue acquisition (starts Day 30)

  • Objective: Leads (Meta collects venue email + phone via native form)
  • Audience: Small business owners, restaurant owners, bar owners, event coordinators. Location: 25-mile radius around Thousand Oaks, expanding to LA proper by Day 45. Age 30–65. Interests: "restaurant management", "small business", "live music" (combined).
  • Budget: $15/day × 30 days = $450
  • Creative: 3 ad variants (see §11 for copy). Photo of a real gig at 14 Cannons overlaid with headline text.
  • Landing page: gigsfill.com/venues (a dedicated LP focused on venues — build if not exists)

Campaign B — Artist acquisition (starts Day 30)

  • Objective: Traffic → signup
  • Audience: Musicians, DJs, comedians. Location: LA metro + Ventura County. Age 21–55. Interests: "musical instruments", "band member", "gigging musician", "open mic", "singer-songwriter". Use Meta's "detailed targeting" and layer 2–3 of these.
  • Budget: $15/day × 30 days = $450
  • Creative: Reel format (repurpose your Friday Reel). Copy: "Get paid within 5 business days after your gig. Sign up free →"

Campaign C — Retargeting (starts Day 40)

  • Objective: Conversions
  • Audience: Website visitors last 30 days who did NOT sign up (Meta Pixel handles this)
  • Budget: $8/day × 20 days = $160
  • Creative: Testimonial video from a venue owner. Copy: "The bar down the street already switched. See why → gigsfill.com"

Google Ads (starts Day 45)

Search intent is gold for you — venue owners actively Googling "how to book a band for my restaurant" are hot leads.

  • Ad group 1: "book live music", "book a band", "book musicians", "hire a band for [venue type]". $15/day for 15 days = $225.
  • Ad group 2 (artist-side, lower priority): "how to get paid gigs", "gig booking for musicians". $5/day for 15 days = $75.
  • Keyword modifiers: "Ventura", "Los Angeles", "Thousand Oaks" — geo-lock so you're not paying for Denver traffic.

Total paid ad budget for Phase 3:

$1,360 total across 30 days. Adjust weekly based on cost per signup. If a channel gives you signups under $15 each, double budget. If it's over $50, cut.

7 · Phase 4 — PR & Partnerships (Days 45–90)

Free press and word-of-mouth from trusted institutions is what pushes you from "another SaaS" to "the platform everyone in the scene is using."

Press outlets to pitch (in priority order)

  1. Ventura County Star (VC Star) — vcstar.com. Angle: local entrepreneur. Reporter: Robin Gonzales or business editor. Email pitch templated in §10.
  2. LA Weekly music section — laweekly.com. Angle: LA music scene infrastructure. Higher bar; pitch after you have real numbers (~Day 60).
  3. Ventura Music Life blog and any local music bloggers. Free content for them; free reach for you.
  4. Restaurant Owner Magazine / Nation's Restaurant News — pitch when you have restaurant/bar case studies.
  5. The Music Business Podcast, Musicians Tip Jar, Bandzoogle Podcast — pitch as a guest. Target 2 podcast appearances by Day 90.

Partnership targets

  • CSU Channel Islands Music Program — 200+ music students, all future gigging artists. Offer: free platform + a workshop on "How to book paid gigs as a working musician"
  • Local music schools — School of Rock, McCabe's, private teachers. Referral partnership: they mention you to graduating students; you mention them in your onboarding as "check these out to level up."
  • Ventura + Thousand Oaks + LA Chambers of Commerce — venue owners are in these. Sponsor one small event ($250–500) — get a booth or 5-min pitch.
  • Sam Ash / Guitar Center store locations in Ventura + LA — leave business cards + flyers at the counter. Store managers usually say yes if you buy them a coffee.
  • Ventura County Coast (tourism board) — they promote local venues to tourists. Offer them a widget/embed showing "live music this weekend" — great for their site, great backlink for you.

Pivot triggers

  • Red on "% gigs booked within 7d" → your artist supply is thin. Freeze venue outreach for 5 days, spend that week on artist recruitment (DMs + local music school outreach). Everything else waits.
  • Red on "Avg cost per venue signup" → paid ads aren't working. Kill them and 2x your in-person outreach. Not every product wins on paid ads — many marketplaces launch entirely through personal networks.
  • Green across the board for 3+ weeks → increase paid ad budget by 50%. Expand geographic radius by 10 miles.

Final note — the one thing that matters

Every marketplace launch fails or wins on the same axis: are you willing to do things that don't scale for the first 60 days?

Personally visit 40 venues. DM 200 artists. Follow up 3 times each. Attend gigs to shoot content. Text venue owners about their Friday. Answer every early signup within an hour. Show up at the Ventura Chamber mixer.

You have every technical piece in place. The product works. The Stripe pipeline works. The email notifications work. The demo cheat sheet exists. The 1099-K auto-issues. All the code is done.

What's left is you, in person, in the local music scene, one venue and one musician at a time, for 90 days. That's the whole strategy.

Then the flywheel spins.
